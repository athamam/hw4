# Campus Customs — Design (Problem 10)

Goal: make the site feel like a real, premium Campus Customs storefront that invites
shoppers to linger and buy — while honoring the brand's pink theme.

## What changed

**Typography & hierarchy.** Added a two-font system: **Fraunces** (an elegant display
serif) for headlines, brand, prices, and product names, paired with **Plus Jakarta
Sans** for body text. Headlines are large with a tight, confident scale; section
titles carry a short gradient underline; the hero headline has an italic pink accent.
*Why:* a distinct voice reads as a curated boutique, not a template — it builds trust
and makes products feel worth the price.

**Color & depth.** A layered pink palette (blush → hot pink → plum) over a warm cream
canvas, with soft radial glows, gradient buttons, and a deep-plum footer.
*Why:* a cohesive, branded world feels professional and keeps attention on the gear;
depth and warmth feel more inviting than a flat page.

**Motion.** Tasteful, purposeful animation: hero fade-up and floating gradient blobs, a
scrolling announcement marquee, staggered product-card entrances, image-zoom and lift
on hover, a glowing/pulsing chat button, buttons with a shine sweep, and a nav bar that
gains a shadow on scroll. All disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
*Why:* subtle movement signals a polished, alive store and guides the eye; hover
feedback makes browsing feel responsive and tactile, encouraging exploration.

**Product presentation.** Cards got rounded corners, soft shadows, image zoom-on-hover,
display-font names, bold price, and a clear sold-out badge. The detail page uses a large
sticky image, prominent price, and chip-based colors/sizes/tags.
*Why:* confident, consistent product cards make the catalogue feel shoppable and
premium, nudging clicks into detail views and carts.

**Chat feel.** The assistant now has a branded gradient header with a "CC" avatar and a
green "online" dot, pop-in message bubbles with tails, animated typing dots, pill-shaped
starter suggestions, and a smooth panel open animation.
*Why:* a friendly, human-feeling assistant lowers the barrier to asking for help, which
drives guided discovery and conversions.

## Why it should help the business
A storefront that looks trustworthy and feels alive keeps shoppers on the page longer,
makes products look desirable, and makes the assistant approachable — more browsing,
more chat engagement, and ultimately more purchases.
